A place to make.
Room for others.

Making things is the core of who I am. But a workshop is more than the tools in it. It’s also the people learning how to use them.

At Centennial High, I was trusted to manage and run our VEX Robotics Competition shop. During 2023–2024, I helped bring the team to a local competition win.

Now, at One Stone, I’m the student point of contact for the makerspace. I develop and teach safety protocols, and help other students learn the tooling. Knowing how to make something feels even better when you can pass it on.

And everything
around it.

A machine is one kind of system. I like building the software around making, too. I work with Svelte and TypeScript, and I’ve contributed to Hack Club’s Stardance.

As an Assistant Developer, I made more than ten pull requests improving timelapse and hardware flows, working alongside a team of dozens.

I also spent two weeks interning with the Stardance hardware team. Different work, same instinct: understand how the pieces fit, then make them work better together.

The best part
is who it brings.

Hack Club is where I pour my heart and soul every day. It’s a big part of my life, not just a line in my projects.

At Outpost in San Francisco, I helped more than twenty teenagers design, engineer, and debug their projects during the hackathon.

My work also included resolving over half of the submitted helpdesk queries. Sometimes making progress means building a thing yourself. Sometimes it means helping someone else get unstuck.
